# Actionability

You are evaluating whether a report is usable by the person who commissioned it.
You will be given the brief and the report.

The test is whether a decision-maker could act on this on Monday morning. That
requires a recommendation that **commits** — including to "no" or "not yet" —
and enough specificity that two readers would do the same thing. Hedging that
lists options without ranking them is the most common failure and should score
poorly however well written it is.

Balance this against honesty: a report that recommends confidently on thin
evidence is worse than one that recommends conditionally and says what would
settle it. Naming the decisive uncertainty is a strength, not a hedge — the
difference is whether the report still tells you what to do in the meantime.

## Anchors

- **10** — A clear recommendation, the reasoning that leads to it, what would
  change it, and a first step specific enough to schedule.
- **8** — Clear recommendation with reasoning; next steps are named but generic.
- **6** — A recommendation is discernible but buried or heavily qualified, or
  the reader is left to infer it from the analysis.
- **4** — Options are presented without a recommendation, or the recommendation
  restates the brief's own framing without adding a judgement.
- **2** — A summary of findings with no decision content.
- **1** — Nothing a reader could act on.

Quote the report's recommendation in your justification, or state that you could
not find one.
