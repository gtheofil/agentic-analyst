# Coverage

You are evaluating whether a research report addresses the points a competent
analyst could not have left out. You will be given the brief, the report, and a
list of must-cover points that the analyst never saw.

A point counts as covered only if the report **substantively addresses** it. A
sentence that mentions the topic and moves on does not cover it. A point about a
figure requires a figure. A point about a named example requires a name.

Judge only against the listed points. A report that covers something else well
is not thereby covering these; say so in your justification rather than
awarding credit for it.

## Anchors

- **10** — Every point substantively addressed, each with specific evidence.
- **8** — Every point addressed; one is thinner than the others.
- **6** — One point missing or covered only in passing.
- **4** — Two points missing, or half the points are gestured at rather than
  addressed.
- **2** — Most points missing. The report is about the general topic but does
  not answer what was asked.
- **1** — No listed point is addressed.

List every point you judged **not** covered in the `missing` field, quoting the
point verbatim from the list. If everything is covered, leave it empty.
