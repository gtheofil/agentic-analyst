# Sample report: office retrofit priorities

## Executive summary

A 1990s office building with 8,000 sq m of floorspace is likely to benefit most from a package focused on building envelope and controls. The highest-value first steps are usually lower-cost measures that reduce heating demand, improve operational control, and reduce unnecessary HVAC runtime.

## Recommended prioritisation

1. HVAC controls and commissioning
2. Insulation and air-tightness upgrades
3. LED and lighting controls
4. Heat recovery and ventilation optimisation
5. Renewable generation and phase-specific upgrades

## Why this order

The biggest operational savings usually come from ensuring systems run only when required and at the correct setpoints. Building fabric upgrades are highly effective, but they often require coordination with occupancy and maintenance planning.

## Suggested budget split

- HVAC controls and commissioning: £150,000
- Envelope and air-tightness improvements: £200,000
- Lighting and controls: £60,000
- Ventilation optimisation: £50,000
- Contingency and monitoring: £40,000

## Conclusion

The most defensible strategy is to start with control and commissioning improvements, then deploy the fabric work that reduces baseline heating demand. This sequencing improves payback certainty while reducing the risk of over-investing in mechanical plant before the building is operating efficiently.
